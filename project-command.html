The problem

Every project starts the same way. A contractor submits a BOQ, and someone on your team spends 2 to 4 hours retyping line items into your own template. It is slow, it is where errors come in (transposed quantities, missed items, wrong groupings), and it is rarely billable. Tenders and site reports carry the same kind of routine capture.

What it does

BOQs

Upload a contractor's BOQ as a PDF or spreadsheet. Project Command maps every line into your firm's own template in minutes instead of 2 to 4 hours. Once the BOQ is live, variations, cost movements (contract, revised and final), meetings and the planned against actual timeline are tracked in the context of that BOQ.

Tenders

Tender documents and submissions kept with the project they belong to, alongside its BOQ.

Site reports

Progress reporting tied to the same project record, with snapshots you can export for stakeholders.

Confidence flagging

“It asks before it assumes.”

BOQs are contractual documents, so accuracy comes first. Every automated match carries a confidence level. Low-confidence lines are not pushed through: they go to a QS for review, which is a short check instead of a full rebuild. The QS confirms or corrects them, and the BOQ goes live only after that review.

Who it's for

  • Quantity surveying practices running several projects at once.
  • Small and mid-sized contractors without spare hands for manual capture.
  • Project teams that need one record of variations and costs.
  • Firms that use their own templates and want software that works with how they already operate.

How it works

  1. You upload a contractor's BOQ, in whatever format it arrived.
  2. Project Command maps it to your template and gives every line a confidence level.
  3. Your QS reviews the flagged lines, confirms them or corrects them.
  4. The BOQ goes live, ready for pricing.
  5. As the project moves, you log variations, costs, meetings and timeline updates against it.
  6. You export reports whenever stakeholders need them.
